How the scoring changes your draft

Four settings in this league push draft value around more than a "standard" cheat sheet accounts for.

RUSH & REC — 1 PT / 10 YDS (EQUAL)

Receiving yards score at the same rate as rushing yards here. A back who catches passes isn't just picking up cheap PPR points — his receiving yards are worth exactly as much as his rushing yards. Dual-threat RBs are a tier above pure between-the-tackles runners.

PASSING — 1 PT / 25 YDS, 4 PT TD, −1 INT

Quarterback scoring is modest, and with only one QB slot and no superflex, there's no premium for hoarding passers. Do not reach for QB — the position is replaceable in a 10-team, 1-QB league.

KICKER — DISTANCE WEIGHTED (50+ = 5 PT)

Field goals scale up with distance instead of paying flat. Target a kicker on a high-volume, long-range-capable offense over a purely "accurate chip-shot" kicker.

DEFENSE — SHUTOUT = 10, 21–27 = 0, 35+ = −4

The points-allowed bands swing hard. A defense's Week 1 matchup matters more than its name-brand reputation — stream aggressively rather than drafting a "top" defense too early.

Position priority

With 2 RB + 2 WR + 1 FLEX (RB/WR/TE) starting every week, this roster wants RB/WR depth more than anything else — lean RB-WR early, let QB and TE come to you.

RB Rounds 1–6, again 9–12

Your highest-priority position. Two starting slots plus flex eligibility means 3 useful RBs minimum, and the position breaks (injury, committee backfields) more than any other. Take the best one on the board early; keep taking them into the middle rounds for depth and bye-week cover.

Tier 1Bell-cow, 18+ touches/game, involved in the passing game — the safest early picks in this format.
Tier 2Clear lead back but timeshare risk, or elite receiving back in a committee.
Tier 3+Handcuffs and change-of-pace backs with standalone flex value — load up rounds 9–12.
WR Rounds 1–7

Deep position, mix it in with RB from round 1 rather than punting it early. Half-PPR means target volume matters, but touchdown equity and yards still carry real weight — don't overvalue a high-target, low-yardage possession receiver just for catches.

Tier 1Clear #1 option on his own offense with 130+ target pace.
Tier 2Strong WR2s, or high-upside young receivers entering a bigger role.
Tier 3+Boom/bust flex options, target-earners on run-heavy offenses.
TE Elite tier only in 4–6, otherwise 8–11

Only one TE slot (it also fills the FLEX if you're stacked at RB/WR). The gap between the true elite tier and the replaceable middle tier is enormous at this position — either pay for a top option early, or ignore it entirely until round 8+.

Tier 1Weekly top-3 finisher, functions like a WR1 — rare, worth an early pick if he's there.
Everyone elseLargely interchangeable streaming options — wait, don't reach.
QB Rounds 7–10

One starting slot, no superflex, half-PPR knocks down passing yardage value — this is the position to be most patient at. A QB drafted round 8 scores nearly as well as one drafted round 3 in a league shaped like this.

Rushing QBsPriority tier — rushing yards/TDs score at the same rich rate as RB/WR production.
Pure passersFine as a value pick late, not worth reaching for given 25 yds/pt.
K / DEF Final 2 rounds

Don't draft either before round 16. Take one kicker with strong leg range for the distance-weighted scoring, and one defense with the best Week 1 matchup on the board — both positions are freely available on waivers all season if this week's picks flame out.
